## 1. Hedge lexicon — English

Words and phrases that drain force. Cut or replace most of them.

**Opinion-softeners:** I think, I feel like, I believe, in my opinion, it seems, arguably,
it could be argued, one might say, I'd argue, personally.

**Probability hedges:** maybe, perhaps, possibly, probably, sort of, kind of, somewhat,
a bit, fairly, rather, quite.

**Scope-shrinkers:** generally, usually, in most cases, often, tends to, can be, may be,
in some sense, to some extent, for the most part.

**Pre-concessions (the killer ones):** of course, admittedly, to be fair, that said,
obviously not always, while it's true that, I know this is controversial but, not gonna
lie. These pre-apologize for the claim before anyone attacks it. Delete on sight unless
the concession is doing real argumentative work.

**Apology tails:** but it depends, but your mileage may vary, but everyone's different,
but I could be wrong, just my two cents.

Note: hedges aren't evil. A precise qualifier that genuinely scopes a claim ("on projects
with more than one contributor") is *strength*, not weakness — it makes the claim sharper
and more defensible. Cut the vague hedges; keep the precise ones.

## 2. Hedge lexicon — Spanish

**Suavizadores de opinión:** creo que, me parece que, pienso que, en mi opinión, diría que,
podría decirse, se podría argumentar, para mí, yo creo.

**Hedges de probabilidad:** quizá, quizás, tal vez, puede que, igual, a lo mejor,
posiblemente, probablemente, más o menos, un poco, bastante, algo.

**Reductores de alcance:** en general, normalmente, en la mayoría de casos, suele,
tiende a, puede ser, en cierto sentido, hasta cierto punto, por lo general.

**Pre-concesiones (las que más matan):** por supuesto, hay que reconocer que, todo sea
dicho, dicho esto, evidentemente no siempre, si bien es cierto que, sé que esto es
polémico pero, no te voy a engañar. Piden perdón por la tesis antes de que nadie ataque.
Bórralas salvo que la concesión aporte argumento de verdad.

**Coletillas de disculpa:** pero depende, pero cada caso es un mundo, pero cada uno es
diferente, pero puedo estar equivocado, es solo mi opinión.

Igual que en inglés: un matiz preciso que acota de verdad la afirmación ("en proyectos con
más de un colaborador") suma filo, no lo resta. Corta los hedges vagos; conserva los
precisos.

## 3. Extended move catalog

Beyond the five core moves in SKILL.md, these refinements help:

- **Name the enemy, not the people.** Target ideas, practices, or products ("the
  'tests slow you down' belief"), not individuals. Sharper *and* it doesn't make you a jerk.
- **Use the second person.** "You quit on iteration 2" implicates the reader and pulls
  them in. Far stickier than the abstract third person.
- **Invert the common wisdom.** If everyone says X, the take "X is exactly backwards, and
  here's the mechanism" is high-energy — but only run it if you can actually show the
  mechanism. Inversion without a reason is just contrarianism.
- **Quantify the stake.** "$30/mo", "4 hours to 15 minutes", "600 commits". Numbers make a
  claim concrete and harder to dismiss.
- **End on the claim, not the caveat.** The last sentence is what people screenshot. Put
  the strongest line there, never the disclaimer.

## 4. Hook templates

Openers that create tension fast. Use sparingly and only when the claim earns it:

- "Unpopular opinion: [claim]." (Overused; only if the opinion really is unpopular.)
- "[Common belief] is wrong. Here's why: [mechanism]."
- "Stop [common practice]. [Consequence]."
- "Nobody wants to admit it, but [claim]."
- "The reason [bad outcome] keeps happening is [blunt cause]."
- "[Thing you pay for] has [time horizon] left." (Scarcity + target.)
- "If you [common behavior], [claim about what that reveals]."

A hook is a promise. Don't write a hook the body can't pay off — that's the fastest way to
look like an empty hot-take account.

## 5. Anti-patterns — what NOT to produce

These are the failure modes that make sharpening backfire. Avoid every one:

- **Manufactured contrarianism.** Taking the opposite of consensus purely for reach, with
  no real conviction behind it. Reads as performative; collapses under one good reply.
- **The false dichotomy.** "Either you use X or you're not a serious engineer." Cheap edge,
  intellectually dishonest, invites pile-ons rather than respect.
- **The strawman.** Sharpening your claim by misrepresenting the other side. Feels powerful,
  destroys credibility the moment someone quotes the real position.
- **Ragebait with no payoff.** A spicy headline over an empty or obvious body. The dunk
  count is short-term; the reputation cost is not.
- **Borrowed conviction.** Adopting someone else's hot take (a Theo, a Hashimoto) in their
  cadence rather than finding the author's own. The audience can tell when the voice isn't
  yours. The goal is the author's strongest *real* opinion, in the author's *own* voice —
  not an impression of a louder person.
- **Punching down.** Edge aimed at junior people, beginners, or the vulnerable isn't bold,
  it's just mean, and it ages badly.

When in doubt, prefer the take that the author could defend calmly in a reply thread three
days later over the one that gets the most reposts today.
